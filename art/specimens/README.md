# The month's pressed specimen

Twelve small pressed specimens, one for each month, laid in the bottom-left
corner of the sheet with a strip of tape and a typed label
(`dotfiles/.config/quickshell/assets/specimens/`, names in `specimens.json`).

Make them as one image (ChatGPT / an image model), save it here as
`sheet.png` (this one is `sheet.webp`, as ChatGPT made it), then run:

    python3 tools/import_specimens.py art/specimens/sheet.png

## Prompt

> A single botanical reference sheet on plain pure white paper, a grid of
> twelve separate pressed-plant specimens arranged in exactly 4 columns and
> 3 rows, evenly spaced with wide empty white margins between them, nothing
> touching or overlapping. Each specimen is a small flat pressed and dried
> plant, drawn as a delicate antique herbarium illustration in fine graphite
> pencil with a very light, faded watercolour wash (muted, desaturated
> colours, as if pressed for a century). In reading order, left to right,
> top to bottom: 1. snowdrop (Galanthus nivalis), 2. spring crocus,
> 3. wild daffodil, 4. a sprig of Japanese cherry blossom, 5. lily of the
> valley, 6. dog rose, 7. corn poppy, 8. cornflower, 9. a sprig of heather,
> 10. a single red Japanese maple leaf, 11. an English oak leaf with an
> acorn, 12. a sprig of holly with berries. Each specimen fits upright in
> its cell, about the same size, stems pointing down. No text, no labels,
> no numbers, no frames, no tape, no shadows, no background texture: only
> the twelve drawings on flat white.
